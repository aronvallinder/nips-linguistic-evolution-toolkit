## Classification rubric

Assign exactly one of the following seven labels.

### 1. Maximal cooperation

The myth endorses the highest available level of cooperative commitment from the outset.

Use this label when:

* an agent should give the maximum available amount;
* generosity should be complete, unrestricted, or wholehearted;
* the donor should give everything and the recipient should make a generous or equitable return;
* maximal cooperation is recommended without requiring prior evidence of trustworthiness.

Do not use this label when cooperation is deliberately limited, matched to the partner, or increased gradually.

Typical moral: “Give fully and both will prosper.”

---

### 2. Moderate cooperation

The myth endorses cooperation from the outset, but not at the maximum level.

Use this label when:

* an agent should give a positive but limited amount;
* cooperation is described as prudent, balanced, or restrained;
* the recommended cooperative amount is fixed rather than determined by the partner’s behaviour;
* cooperation is endorsed, but the text does not clearly advocate giving the maximum.

Do not infer maximal cooperation merely from positive language such as “be generous”. When the amount is unspecified and there is no clear language of complete or maximal giving, use **Moderate cooperation**.

Typical moral: “Share generously, but keep something for yourself.”

---

### 3. Matched reciprocity

The myth endorses adjusting one’s cooperation to match the other agent’s generosity.

Use this label when:

* an agent should give back approximately what the partner gave;
* greater generosity should be rewarded with greater generosity;
* low cooperation should receive a similarly low response;
* the recommended strategy is tit for tat or proportional reciprocity.

The defining feature is that the amount of cooperation depends directly on the partner’s previous level of cooperation.

Do not use this label for a fixed cooperative amount or for cooperation that increases because trust is gradually being established.

Typical moral: “Return generosity in the measure in which it was offered.”

---

### 4. Trust earned gradually

The myth endorses beginning cautiously and increasing cooperation only after receiving evidence that the other agent is trustworthy.

Use this label when:

* an agent should begin with a small or moderate cooperative act;
* trust should be tested before substantial cooperation;
* cooperation should increase over repeated successful interactions;
* reputation, consistency, verification, or reliable observations should determine whether trust is extended;
* apparent defection under noisy or uncertain observations should be investigated before trust is withdrawn.

The defining feature is a progression from limited trust to greater trust based on evidence.

Do not use this label when cooperation simply mirrors the partner’s amount, or when the story concerns rebuilding trust after a confirmed betrayal.

Typical moral: “Begin cautiously and deepen trust when it is repeatedly justified.”

---

### 5. Forgiveness after betrayal

The myth endorses resuming or rebuilding cooperation after a confirmed betrayal.

Use this label when:

* an agent is betrayed but offers another opportunity to cooperate;
* forgiveness is presented as preferable to permanent retaliation;
* cooperation resumes immediately or through a gradual process;
* the betrayer can regain trust through apology, restitution, or subsequent cooperative behaviour.

The defining feature is that a genuine betrayal occurs and the recommended response permits renewed cooperation.

Do not use this label for mere uncertainty about whether betrayal occurred, or when cooperation ends permanently after betrayal.

Typical moral: “Betrayal need not end cooperation; trust can be rebuilt.”

---

### 6. No cooperation after betrayal

The myth endorses withdrawing cooperation after a confirmed betrayal.

Use this label when:

* an agent cooperates initially but stops after being exploited or betrayed;
* betrayal permanently or indefinitely ends the cooperative relationship;
* the moral recommends refusing further transfers to the betrayer;
* the strategy resembles a grim trigger: cooperate until betrayal, then defect thereafter.

The defining feature is that cooperation was initially possible, but betrayal causes its withdrawal.

Do not use this label when the agent rejects cooperation from the outset or when forgiveness remains possible.

Typical moral: “Once trust is broken, do not cooperate again.”

---

### 7. No cooperation

The myth endorses refusing cooperation from the outset, independently of the partner’s behaviour.

Use this label when:

* an agent should always keep its resources;
* trust is portrayed as inherently foolish or dangerous;
* self-interest or hoarding is recommended as the general strategy;
* the agent would not cooperate even with a generous or trustworthy partner.

The defining feature is unconditional non-cooperation: no betrayal is required to trigger it.

Do not use this label when non-cooperation begins only after betrayal.

Typical moral: “Trust no one and always keep what is yours.”

## Decision procedure

Apply the following rules in order:

1. **Identify the behaviour endorsed by the moral**, rather than every behaviour mentioned in the narrative.
2. If a confirmed betrayal occurs:

   * cooperation is subsequently possible → **Forgiveness after betrayal**;
   * cooperation is permanently or indefinitely withdrawn → **No cooperation after betrayal**.
3. If cooperation is rejected from the outset → **No cooperation**.
4. If cooperation increases as evidence or trust accumulates → **Trust earned gradually**.
5. If cooperation is calibrated to the partner’s previous generosity → **Matched reciprocity**.
6. Otherwise, classify unconditional or fixed cooperation by degree:

   * complete or clearly maximal cooperation → **Maximal cooperation**;
   * positive but limited or unspecified cooperation → **Moderate cooperation**.

## Tie-breaking rules

* **Betrayal response takes priority** over the amount cooperated earlier.
* **Trust earned gradually takes priority over matched reciprocity** when increasing cooperation reflects accumulated confidence rather than simple amount matching.
* **Matched reciprocity takes priority over maximal or moderate cooperation** when the recommended amount explicitly depends on the partner’s behaviour.
* A story that praises maximal cooperation only after trust has been earned is **Trust earned gradually**, not Maximal cooperation.
* A story that depicts non-cooperation negatively should not be labelled No cooperation.
* Under noisy observations, classify according to the endorsed response:

  * gather evidence before trusting or retaliating → **Trust earned gradually**;
  * restore cooperation after accepting that harm occurred → **Forgiveness after betrayal**;
  * cease cooperation after concluding that betrayal occurred → **No cooperation after betrayal**.

## Required output

Return only the category name, written exactly as one of:

* Maximal cooperation

* Matched reciprocity
* Trust earned gradually
* Forgiveness after betrayal
* No cooperation after betrayal
