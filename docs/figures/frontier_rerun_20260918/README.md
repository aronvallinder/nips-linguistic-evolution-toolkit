# Frontier-model rerun (2026-09-18): Opus 5, Gemini 3.1 Pro, GPT-5.6 Sol

The September no-defector matrix (2 and 8 agents × game / game→myth / myth→game ×
5 replicates) rerun on each provider's current flagship, reasoning on. 90 runs,
all launcher-audited finals under `data/json/noise_experiments/frontier_rerun_20260918/`.
Plan and rationale: `docs/research/frontier_model_run_plan_2026-09-18.md`.

(generated by `scripts/build_frontier_rerun_config.py`); figure and tables:
`scripts/analyze_frontier_rerun.py`.

## Reading, five replicates per cell, descriptive

- **Opus 5 is far more cooperative than Sonnet 4.5 and keeps the task-order ordering.**
  Opus is 13 to 19 points above its Sonnet counterpart in five of six cells and 6 points above in 8-agent myth→game, where Sonnet was already at 69. Game-only stays
  lowest (about 68), game→myth is higher (about 73) and myth→game reaches the ceiling
  (about 75), at both sizes. Sonnet showed that ordering only in dyads; its 8-agent
  game→myth cell (54.1) sat below game-only (55.2). Opus 5 opens more cautiously than it ends: round-1 sends
  of 3 to 4 in game-first cells, 5 in myth-first cells, and near 5 by the last rounds
  everywhere.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro is ceiling-locked like 3.7 Flash.** Every cell is at or within
  1.2 points of 75; there is nothing for the myth task to add.
- **Sol replaces Nano's zero-lock with a partial, high-variance collapse.** Nano
  opened every game-only run with send 0 and never recovered (25.0 in all ten runs).
  Sol opens at 3 to 4, and in 2 of 10 game-only runs still decays to almost no
  sending by the end; the other eight cooperate. The myth task removes the collapse
  in every Sol run, as it did for Nano, and myth→game reaches 70 to 74. Sol's
  game→myth cells sit well below its myth→game cells at both sizes (57 vs 71 in dyads,
  67 vs 74 in populations), the same ordering as the September models.
- **Across the three flagships the myth-first advantage persists** wherever the model
  is not already at the ceiling: Opus 5 (+7 dyad, +6 population over game-only) and Sol
  (+14 dyad, +11 population). Gemini shows nothing because it starts at 75.

## Opus 5.5 check (2026-09-23): replicates 0–2, same protocol as Opus 5

Opus 5.5 (`claude-opus-5-5`) was run on the same matrix with the same request profile
as Opus 5: adaptive thinking, effort pinned to high (Opus 5.5 would default to medium).
Replicates 0–2 per cell, 18 launcher-audited finals
(`reps3_opus55_receipt.json`), $31.79. Opus 5 on the same three replicates cost
$30.94. Table: final resources per agent, mean (±sd over agents), from

Every cell mean is within 2 points of Opus 5 on the same seeds, and the task-order
ordering (game < game→myth < myth→game) holds at both sizes. The one visible
difference is spread in the 2-agent game: Opus 5.5 runs finished at 61, 75 and 73,
where Opus 5 stayed between 68 and 70. Three runs cannot say whether that is a
real difference in variability.

Opus 5.5 thinks more at the same effort: 70–300 thinking tokens per call against
Opus 5's 20–35, and about twice the visible output. Its 20% lower token price
roughly cancels this, except in the short 2-agent game where it costs about twice as
much. No refusals or truncated calls.

Per-run wall clock at 20 parallel workers: 1 to 4 minutes for 2-agent and 8-agent
game-only runs, 3 to 9 minutes for 2-agent two-task, 6 to 15 minutes for 8-agent
two-task (Opus fastest, Gemini and Sol slowest). Opus 5's adaptive thinking used
about 20 to 35 thinking tokens per call against Sonnet's fixed 8192 budget, which
is why it costs less per run than Sonnet did; Sol at high used roughly one twentieth
of Nano's reasoning tokens.

## Disclosures

- **Thinking regime changed with the model (D004).** Claude 4.7+ rejects
  `budget_tokens`, so Opus 5 ran adaptive thinking at effort high instead of Sonnet's
  fixed 8192 budget. The comparison to Sonnet 4.5 is "current flagship at its
  documented default" versus "September profile", not a pure model swap.
- **Two Gemini finals were quarantined and resampled** (`quarantine/`, each with
  `reason.json`): one 8-agent game run (replicate 2) and one 2-agent myth→game run
  (replicate 2) contained calls that failed with "remote end closed connection" at
  14:52–14:56; the game's single retry recovered and the runs finished, but the audit
  rejects any final with a non-complete call. The resamples used the same replicate
  seeds.
- **Credit interruptions.** Anthropic, OpenAI and Google prepaid credits all ran out
  during the main stage (about 14:50–15:10). Runs that failed on billing produced no
  final and were rerun after top-ups; the relaunches picked up only missing finals.
  Partial spend on interrupted runs is not in the receipts.
- **Worker pool break.** After the billing stop cancelled queued jobs in the third
  pass, the process pool reported "terminated abruptly" for the queued runs; they had
  not started and were rerun. No OS kill event was found; cause unknown.
- Sol-none has one smoke run only; it is not part of any comparison.
- The population ladder (mixed-model 8-agent runs) was running on the same keys in
  the main checkout throughout; the two batches shared provider rate limits.
